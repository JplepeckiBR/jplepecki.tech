An embedded wearable that measures pressure and motion from inside a shoe to
build a picture of how someone walks. It is the flagship of my move from
finance into the hardware/software intersection: real firmware, real sensors,
real people to test with.

## Problem

Gait tells you a lot — rehab progress, fatigue, asymmetry, load — but capturing
it usually means a lab, a force plate, or a motion-capture rig. I wanted the
signal to live where the foot actually is: in the insole.

## Approach

- **Sensing.** Pressure (force-sensitive resistors) across key contact zones,
  plus an IMU for orientation and motion.
- **MCU.** Running on a Seeed XIAO nRF52840 Sense. It started on an Arduino
  board; I moved to the nRF52840 for its size, onboard sensing, and BLE.
- **Build space.** Prototyped and tested at the robotics club at
  [Artisan's Asylum](https://artisansasylum.com/) in Somerville, which gave me a
  bench, tools, and people to sanity-check ideas against.
- **Discovery.** Alongside the hardware I ran structured customer-discovery
  conversations to find who actually needs this.
